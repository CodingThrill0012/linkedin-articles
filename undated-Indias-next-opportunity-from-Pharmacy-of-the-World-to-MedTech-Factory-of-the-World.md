### Can India Become the MedTech Factory of the World?

India is already known as the **Pharmacy of the World**. We manufacture affordable generic medicines, vaccines and formulations at a global scale.

But advanced medical devices including continuous glucose monitors, insulin pumps, infusion systems, biosensors and precision medical electronics still depend heavily on imported components and technology.

This is India’s next major opportunity.

India has approximately **101 million people living with diabetes and another 136 million with prediabetes**. Yet manufacturers face an addressable-market trap: the Type 1 diabetes population requiring continuous insulin delivery is comparatively small, advanced devices remain expensive, insurance coverage is limited and government procurement is uncertain.

The solution is not to manufacture only an insulin pump.

India should build reusable MedTech capabilities in:

-   Biosensors and microfluidics

-   Precision drug delivery

-   Medical-grade materials

-   Safety-critical electronics and firmware

-   Connected patient monitoring

-   Clinical AI and cybersecurity

These capabilities can support insulin pumps, smart pens, chemotherapy infusion systems, neonatal devices, cardiac monitors, dialysis equipment and point-of-care diagnostics.

India should begin with a progressive product ladder: connected glucometers, smart insulin pens, affordable CGMs, infusion devices and eventually automated drug-delivery platforms.

Government can unlock the market through guaranteed procurement, insurance coverage, shared testing infrastructure, clinical-validation corridors and incentives for manufacturing critical components—not merely assembling imported designs.

India’s pharmaceutical expertise, software talent, engineering capability and enormous healthcare demand give us a unique advantage.

We should not aim to become only the cheapest manufacturer of Western medical devices.

We should build **affordable, secure and clinically validated medical technology designed for India and the Global South**.

This mission requires healthcare, biotechnology, manufacturing, digital infrastructure and public policy to work together.
