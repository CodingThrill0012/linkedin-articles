### 

Having witnessed the Northeast’s journey from 2006 to 2026, I see the past decade as a decisive turning point.

For too long, the region was viewed through the narrow lens of distance, difficult terrain and border security. Over the last ten years, that mindset has changed. The Northeast is increasingly being treated as a strategic gateway for India’s economic growth, national integration and Act East ambitions.

### Breaking geographical isolation

The Brahmaputra was not simply a river it was a formidable economic and logistical barrier.

The opening of the 9.15-km Dhola–Sadiya Bridge in 2017 significantly improved connectivity in eastern Assam and strategic access towards Arunachal Pradesh. The Bogibeel rail-cum-road bridge, inaugurated in 2018, connected the Dibrugarh and Dhemaji sides of the Brahmaputra and strengthened both civilian and defence mobility.

The Kumar Bhaskar Varma Setu, opened in February 2026, represents the next stage bringing Guwahati and North Guwahati closer and supporting the emergence of a more integrated twin-city economy.

These bridges do more than reduce travel time. They connect people to hospitals, universities, employment, markets and administrative services.

### Building institutions, not only infrastructure

Physical connectivity becomes truly transformative when it is supported by institutional capacity.

IIT Guwahati was established in 1994, but it has continued expanding as a nationally important research and innovation institution. AIIMS Guwahati, established in 2017 and inaugurated in 2023, has strengthened access to advanced healthcare within the region.

The development of the north bank around these institutions demonstrates an important principle:

> **Infrastructure creates connectivity, but institutions create lasting economic gravity.**

### Development and national security moving together

In the Northeast, economic infrastructure also has strategic importance.

Better bridges, highways, railways, tunnels, airports and communication networks improve everyday life while enabling faster disaster response, border logistics and defence mobility. The region’s geography makes this dual value unavoidable—and essential.

But the Northeast should not be seen only as India’s strategic frontier. It should be recognised as:

-   India’s gateway to Southeast Asia

-   A centre for renewable energy and natural resources

-   A tourism and cultural powerhouse

-   An emerging education, healthcare and technology hub

-   A critical component of India’s long-term national resilience

Development must, of course, remain environmentally responsible and sensitive to the region’s communities, cultures and ecological wealth. Speed without sustainability would create a different form of vulnerability.

The larger lesson is clear:

> **The Northeast is not the last mile of India. It is the first mile of India’s journey towards Southeast Asia and an essential pillar of Viksit Bharat 2047.**

**Northeast rising means India rising.**
