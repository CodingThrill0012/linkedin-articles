## 

**By Divyendu Bhatt**

**846 ransomware victims listed in the first 24 days of October 2026.** **151,000+ failed logins in one AI-assisted campaign.** **2,900 authentication attempts per minute.**

For years cybersecurity kept saying:

**Winter is coming.**

No.

**Winter has come.**

Attackers are automated, identity-driven, API-native and increasingly agentic.

The problem is simple:

**Humans cannot scale linearly against machines.**

But replacing humans with autonomous agents is not the answer either.

The future is a **Human–Agent Cyber Defence System**.

### Quick Calculation: A Mid-Sized Digital Bank

Assume:

**Revenue:** \~$3.4B **Employees:** \~6,000 **Technology budget:** \~$270M **Cybersecurity team:** \~60–70 people **Cyber budget:** \~$25–35M

Now look at the SOC.

A bank may have only **12–15 dedicated SOC professionals**.

To maintain lean 24×7 coverage:

**2 analysts × 3 shifts = 6 staffed positions**

Add weekends, leave, training, sickness and rotation:

**6 × 1.45 ≈ 9 FTE**

So roughly **9 people may be consumed just maintaining 24×7 coverage**.

That is before serious threat hunting, detection engineering, identity investigation, cloud security, attack-path analysis and incident-response improvement.

### Then Come the Agents

An Intelligent SOC could shift part of that repetitive workload to specialised agents.

Perhaps:

**9 FTE of continuous operational workload → 4–6 human FTE + agents**

But this does **not** mean cybersecurity becomes cheaper.

Now the bank needs:

AI-security engineering, agent orchestration, machine identities, model costs, data pipelines, agent monitoring, trust controls and deterministic policy enforcement.

So the organisation may move from:

**65 cyber professionals → 65–70**

And perhaps:

**$28M cyber budget → $32–35M**

That is not necessarily bad economics.

Because the objective should not be:

**Reduce cybersecurity headcount.**

The objective should be:

**Increase defensive capacity.**

A traditional SOC may have:

**12 humans processing alerts.**

An Intelligent SOC could have:

**6 humans supervising 50–100 specialised defensive agents and automated processes.**

That changes the equation.

**Traditional SOC:** Defensive Capacity ≈ Human Headcount

**Intelligent SOC:** Defensive Capacity ≈ Humans × Machine Scale × Automation × Context

Subject to:

**Authority Boundaries + Verification + Human Escalation**

So the future SOC may cost **more**, not less.

But it may defend dramatically more attack surface.

That is why the better metric is not:

**Cybersecurity cost per employee.**

It is:

## Cost per Unit of Defended Digital Risk

People guard the mission. Agents guard the scale. Trust controls guard the agents.

**Winter has come.**

The question for CISOs, CIOs and boards is no longer:

**How many more people do we hire to guard the Wall?**

It is:

**What should humans own, what should agents own, and what must never be delegated?**

If you are designing a SOC budget for 2027, this may be one of the most important operating-model decisions you make.

**Should Agentic SOC be measured by headcount reduction, or by defensive capacity gained per dollar spent?**
