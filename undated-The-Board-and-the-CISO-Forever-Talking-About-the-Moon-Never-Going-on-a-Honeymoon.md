**“I show them the moon; they keep looking at my finger.”**

This captures the growing friction between CISOs and boards when discussing **AI risk, controls, and cost**.

The CISO sees the bigger picture:

-   Shadow AI and uncontrolled adoption

-   Data leakage and prompt injection

-   Manipulated or unreliable outputs

-   Weak agent identity and excessive permissions

-   Regulatory, privacy, legal, and reputational exposure

-   AI actions that cannot be explained, traced, or verified

The board often sees the control cost:

**Why is it so expensive?** **Do we need another security layer?** **Will it slow deployment?** **What is the immediate ROI?**

The board sees the cost of control. The CISO sees the cost of uncontrolled autonomy.

An inadequately governed AI agent can make decisions, invoke tools, modify workflows, generate code, approve transactions, and influence customers at machine speed and enterprise scale.

The real comparison is not: **Control cost versus zero cost**

It is: **Control cost versus the financial, operational, regulatory, and reputational impact of AI failure.**

AI controls must be **proportionate, measurable, automated where possible, and aligned with business criticality.**

But removing controls does not eliminate cost.It postpones it and postponed security cost often returns as incident cost, recovery cost, regulatory cost, and loss of trust.

**The finger is the security control. The moon is business resilience, trust, and sustainable AI adoption.**

A mature board should ask not only:

**“How much will AI security cost?”**

But also:

**“What could uncontrolled AI cost us?”**
