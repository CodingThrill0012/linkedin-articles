Have you ever heard of the **Bouba–Kiki effect**? It’s a fascinating cognitive phenomenon: when people see two shapes — one round and soft, the other jagged and sharp — almost everyone calls the round one *“Bouba”* and the sharp one *“Kiki.”*

Our brains naturally **associate shapes, sounds, and emotions** — Bouba feels safe and flowing, Kiki feels risky and alert.

Now, imagine the brain of a **CISO (Chief Information Security Officer)**. It’s constantly oscillating between these two modes:

-   **Bouba Mode:** empathy, communication, trust-building, culture-shaping.

-   **Kiki Mode:** threat detection, incident response, defensive readiness.

A CISO’s effectiveness depends on how well they **balance these cognitive modes** — how they can sense danger (Kiki) yet lead calmly through chaos (Bouba). When that balance is off, the organisation either becomes paranoid or complacent.

### CEOs — Here’s the insight

You’ll know you’ve found the right CISO when you see both:

-   A sharp, analytical mind that detects invisible risks before anyone else.

-   A soft, human mind that wins hearts and builds psychological safety.
