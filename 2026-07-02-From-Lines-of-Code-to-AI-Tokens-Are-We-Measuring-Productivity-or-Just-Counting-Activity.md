<div>

I came across a meme today that made me smile.

A museum called the **"Museum of Meaningless Metrics."**

When I started my career, productivity was measured by **Lines of Code**. Then came **Story Points**, followed by **Pull Requests**. Each generation believed it had finally found the perfect way to measure engineering productivity, yet the best engineers I worked with often wrote less code, deleted more than they added, and quietly made systems faster, simpler, and more secure. The dashboards evolved, but the real value always came from solving problems—not from improving the numbers.

Now, in the AI era, we've found a new metric: **Tokens Consumed**. But are we measuring productivity or simply AI usage? One engineer can burn millions of tokens generating code that never reaches production, while another uses a fraction of that to eliminate a critical security risk or solve a customer problem. Over two decades in technology, I've learned that whenever we confuse **activity** with **impact**, we optimize the wrong thing. Customers don't care how many tokens we consumed—they care that the product is fast, reliable, secure, and solves their problem. Maybe the question isn't

**"How much AI did we use?"**

but

**"What became better because we used AI?"**
