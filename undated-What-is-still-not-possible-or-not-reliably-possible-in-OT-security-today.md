We have made significant progress in asset discovery, network visibility, vulnerability identification and anomaly detection.

Platforms such as Cisco Cyber Vision, Claroty, Nozomi and Dragos can help us see what is connected and what may be vulnerable.

But can today’s OT-security ecosystem reliably answer these questions?

1.  Is the discovered asset correctly represented in the latest engineering drawing?

2.  If no drawing exists, can we reconstruct one from network evidence, field photographs, control logic and equipment documentation?

3.  Was a field modification formally approved or merely implemented during an urgent maintenance activity?

4.  Is the detected vulnerability actually reachable and exploitable in this specific process environment?

5.  What could happen to production, safety, quality and the environment if we patch this asset?

6.  What could happen if we do **not** patch it?

7.  Can we identify the safest remediation sequence across interconnected systems?

8.  Can the proposed change be simulated against actual process dependencies before touching production?

9.  Which vendor, asset owner, safety engineer and production manager must approve the intervention?

10. Does the third-party engineer have access only to the required asset, protocol, task and approved maintenance window?

11. Can every vendor be required to follow the plant owner’s engineering, security and evidence rules from onboarding onward?

12. Can SIEM alerts, vulnerabilities, engineering changes, vendor activities and process consequences be correlated into one operational decision?

13. Can we prove that the approved design still matches the deployed configuration?

14. After remediation, can we prove that risk was reduced without introducing a new operational hazard?

15. Can a CISO see one prioritised view based on cyber risk, safety impact, production loss, regulatory exposure and remediation feasibility?

The OT-security market is becoming very good at telling us:

**“Something may be wrong.”**

The remaining challenge is helping the plant confidently decide:

**“What should we do, who must approve it, when can we do it and how do we know it will not disrupt operations?”**

Perhaps the next stage of OT security is not another monitoring console.

It is an **OT Decision Intelligence and Assurance Layer** connecting cyber telemetry with engineering truth, operational context, vendor governance and safe remediation.

Which of these questions can your current OT-security architecture answer today and which remain unresolved?
