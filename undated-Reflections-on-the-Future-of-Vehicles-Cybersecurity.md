Sunday was super interesting. In Over Golf & Post Golf Session, Coffee with like-minded professionals, our discussion quickly drifted from present-day challenges to the **future of vehicles** — a world where **Software-Defined Vehicles (SDV)**, **Cloud-Native Architectures**, **LEO satellites**, **Quantum Computing**, and **Autonomous AI traffic control** might all converge.

At first, it felt like sci-fi. By the **late 2030s**, when Skynet-like satellite networks are household utilities, cars will no longer be “machines with software” — they’ll be **cloud-driven platforms on wheels**. In such an era, threats that today apply only to critical infrastructure may become risks for **the common man’s commute**. Imagine — traffic lights orchestrated by autonomous AI, your car’s firmware patched via LEO uplinks, and adversaries exploiting **quantum-enabled attacks** against vehicle security systems.

We brainstormed how **automotive cybersecurity** will evolve — from protecting ECUs today to safeguarding **entire cloud-native ecosystems of vehicles tomorrow**. While some of us shared concerns, we also laughed, imagining the absurd:

“What happens when even a common man’s flush has AI?”

It ended on jokes, but beneath the humor is a real signal — the **future of transport is deeply entangled with the future of cybersecurity**.

The way I see it:

-   **Vehicles will be nodes in a global, AI-controlled mobility mesh.**

-   **Quantum + Cloud + AI + LEO** will redefine both opportunity and threat models.

-   **Cybersecurity won’t just protect cars — it will protect entire societies’ movement.**

Crazy? Yes. Far-fetched? Maybe. But every big leap starts with ideas that once felt laughable. Btw, LEO Constellation Attacks are real. The EU already has a Policy. Ponder deeply if my fear for the future is real
