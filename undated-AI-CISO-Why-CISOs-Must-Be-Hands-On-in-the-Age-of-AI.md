### Why CISOs Must Be Hands-On in the Age of AI

> "You can’t defend what you don’t understand. And in the AI era, *understanding* requires rolling up your sleeves." You must know what is behind the walls - Divyendu Bhatt

As artificial intelligence redefines how we build, deploy, and interact with technology, the role of the Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) is undergoing its own transformation. No longer is it sufficient to be a policy enforcer or compliance champion—today’s CISOs must be **hands-on leaders**, especially when AI is part of the stack.

Need more to Absorb?

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 1. AI Doesn’t Behave Like Traditional Software - I am sure you agree

AI systems are probabilistic, dynamic, and often non-transparent. Security challenges like **model drift**, **data poisoning**, **prompt injection**, or **inference leakage** can’t be fully grasped through slide decks or risk matrices. CISOs must understand *how* these systems operate and *where* they can fail. That means getting closer to the code, the data, and the model behavior.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 2. Security Is Now Embedded in DevOps & ML Pipelines

Modern organizations integrate security into CI/CD pipelines and MLOps workflows. From **model signing and scanning**, to **monitoring inference traffic for anomalies**, today’s CISO must be fluent in tooling and capable of working side-by-side with engineers to design secure pipelines. This isn’t micromanagement—it’s **modern leadership**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 3. You Can’t Govern What You Can’t See

The rise of **Shadow AI**—unauthorized AI tools, browser copilots, or rogue LLMs—has created a visibility crisis. You can’t wait for reports. CISOs must get hands-on to:

-   Monitor endpoint behavior

-   Investigate suspicious AI usage patterns

-   Build frameworks to control **BYO-AI** risks

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 4. Credibility with AI Engineers & Product Teams

Security is often seen as a blocker—unless it speaks the same language. A CISO who can open a Jupyter notebook, understand a data labeling function, or troubleshoot an LLM API call builds **instant credibility**. You shift from being a gatekeeper to being a **coach and collaborator**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 5. AI Attacks Evolve Too Fast for Passive Leadership

Attackers are using AI to:

-   Generate polymorphic malware

-   Manipulate model behavior

-   Bypass content moderation A hands-on CISO actively tests defenses, participates in red team exercises, and validates mitigation tools—not after the fact, but **as part of the security lifecycle**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### Food For thought

We are entering an era where **trust in AI systems is the new perimeter**. To secure that trust, CISOs must evolve from abstract strategists to **technical, adaptive leaders**—ready to experiment, break things, and secure them again.

Because in the age of AI, **hands-on is the only way forward, "Lead from the front"**
