## 

I have often worked in organisations during periods of significant operational instability legal and regulatory pressure, M&A, IPO or listing pressure, leadership exits, restructuring, and constantly changing priorities.

In such environments, I learned one thing very clearly:

**Leadership is not about creating more urgency. It is about creating order.**

Over the years, my approach has become simple:

**1. Stabilize before you optimize.** Identify what can genuinely damage the business, customer, people, security or regulatory position. Not every fire deserves the same response.

**2. Create one version of the truth.** Rumours grow when facts are missing. Tell people what is known, what is not known, what is impacted, who owns it, and what happens next.

**3. Protect the team from organisational noise.** People should not have to interpret every leadership change or executive movement. Give them clarity about what they own and what they need to deliver.

**4. Reduce priorities.** During chaos, I use three simple buckets:

**\#Protect \| \#Stabilize \| \#Improve**

**5. Increase communication.** When uncertainty increases, leadership presence must increase. Talk to people. Make decisions faster. Make ownership visible.

**6. Never transfer leadership anxiety to the team.** They need context, decisions and direction not panic.

**7. Keep people connected to purpose.** Especially when others are leaving, people need to understand why their work still matters.

And perhaps the most important lesson:

**Build an operating system that can survive leadership changes.**

Processes, accountability, governance and decision-making should become stronger than any individual leader.

### Lessons I Learned From Leaders

A significant part of this thinking was shaped around a decade ago during my time around Telenor.

I remember @**Sigve Brekke** <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/sigvebrekke/" target="_blank"><strong>https://www.linkedin.com/in/sigvebrekke/</strong></a> coming in and literally beating the drums during a difficult period lifting the energy of the organisation when people needed confidence. I won't ever forget this as I was part of this extraordinary display of leadership, I strongly believe no one could have pulled this like Sigve did it.

From him, I learned that **leadership energy matters**. Sometimes people need to see that their leader still believes the organisation can move forward.

I watched **@Ruza Sabanovic** remain intensely focused on the issues that actually needed solving. To know more about her check <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/ruza-sabanovic/" target="_blank">https://www.linkedin.com/in/ruza-sabanovic/</a>

From her, I learned the importance of **focus, discipline and cutting through noise when everything around you is becoming complicated.**

Those lessons stayed with me.

Over the years, many other leaders have also influenced how I think, behave and lead.

Some taught me through advice.

Some through difficult situations.

Some simply through the way they carried themselves when things were going wrong.

To all those leaders **thank you.**

A large part of the leader I became came from observing you, learning from you, and carrying those lessons into the situations I later had to lead myself.

**Good leaders do more than deliver results.**

**They create other leaders often without even realizing it.**

I was fortunate to learn from leaders who shaped not only how I lead, but also how I think, respond under pressure and work with people. I will always remain grateful to them for the difference they made in my life.Sigve and Ruza are two such leaders, but there are many others I hope to acknowledge in time.

**Some leadership lessons stay with you for a project. Some stay with you for a career. And a few stay with you for life**
