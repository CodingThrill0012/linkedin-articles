Today, if you throw a stone in the air, there is a good chance it will land on an “AI expert.”

The problem is not that more people are learning AI.

**The problem is that too many people are confusing AI usage with AI understanding.**

Using ChatGPT is not AI expertise. Writing prompts is not AI architecture. Building a demo is not production readiness. Creating a policy is not AI governance.

This is **AI Epistemic Pollution** a polluted knowledge environment where shallow understanding, copied frameworks, loud opinions, and synthetic expertise start looking like real capability.

It is driven by **expertise dilution** and the **Dunning-Kruger effect**. ( Don't look at the image and think you know everything)

And the danger is real.

Bad AI thinking becomes bad AI strategy. Bad AI strategy becomes bad AI architecture. Bad AI architecture becomes unsafe products. Unsafe products become business, security, and trust failures.

So how do we solve it?

By moving from **AI excitement to AI evidence**.

Ask better questions:

What have you built? What failed? What did you measure? What risks did you reduce? What broke in production? What controls did you design? What proof do you have?

The next phase of AI does not need more noise.

It needs builders. It needs architects. It needs security thinking. It needs governance. It needs humility. It needs people who understand where AI can fail before they claim where AI can win.

AI will not fail only because models hallucinate.

AI will fail if humans hallucinate expertise.
