# Winter Has Come: The Cybersecurity Budget Problem

Consider a mid-sized digital bank:

**Annual revenue:** \~$3.4B **Employees:** \~6,000 **Estimated technology budget:** \~$270M **Cybersecurity team:** \~60–70 people **Illustrative cyber budget:** \~$25–35M annually

Now ask a simple question:

### Can AI actually reduce the cybersecurity budget?

Probably not.

Take the SOC.

A bank may have only **12–15 people** dedicated to SOC operations.

To maintain even a lean 24×7 model:

**2 analysts × 3 shifts = 6 continuously staffed seats**

Add weekends, leave, training, sickness and rotation:

**6 × 1.45 ≈ 9 FTE**

That means roughly **9 people may be consumed just maintaining 24×7 coverage**.

Before serious threat hunting.

Before detection engineering.

Before identity investigation.

Before cloud security.

Before improving detection logic.

This is why the traditional SOC model struggles.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### Then Come the Agents

The obvious assumption is:

**Agents replace analysts → headcount falls → cyber budget falls.**

I think the opposite may happen.

An Intelligent SOC could reduce repetitive human monitoring from perhaps **9 FTE to 4–6 FTE**.

But the bank now needs:

-   Agent orchestration

-   AI-security engineers

-   Machine identities

-   Agent IAM/PAM

-   Model and inference costs

-   Security data pipelines

-   Deterministic policy controls

-   Agent monitoring and testing

-   Trust and authority governance

So the organisation might move from:

**65 cyber professionals → 65–70 cyber professionals**

And perhaps from:

**$28M annual cyber spend → $32M or even $35M.**

That sounds like failure only if the objective was cost reduction.

It isn't.

The objective is **defensive capacity**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

A traditional SOC might have:

**12 humans investigating alerts.**

An ISOC could have:

**6 humans supervising 50–100 specialised defensive agents and automated processes.**

Those agents continuously work across:

**Identity Cloud Endpoints Applications APIs Threat intelligence Vulnerabilities Fraud signals Attack paths Incident evidence**

So the equation changes.

### Traditional SOC

**Defensive Capacity ≈ Human Headcount**

### Intelligent SOC

**Defensive Capacity ≈ Humans × Machine Scale × Automation × Context**

But subject to:

**Authority Boundaries + Verification + Human Escalation**

That distinction matters.

The future SOC may actually cost **more**.

But the right metric is no longer:

**Cyber cost per employee.**

It should become:

## Cost per Unit of Defended Digital Risk

If a bank increases its cybersecurity budget from:

**$28M → $34M**

but gains:

**5× investigation capacity 24×7 machine-speed detection continuous threat hunting continuous attack-path analysis faster containment better evidence collection**

then security has not become more expensive.

Its **unit economics have improved dramatically.**

That is the real business case for Agentic Cyber Defence.

**People guard the mission.**

**Agents guard the scale.**

**Trust controls guard the agents.**

Winter has come.

The question is no longer:

**How many more people do we hire to guard the Wall?**

It is:

## How much defensive capacity can every cybersecurity dollar buy?
